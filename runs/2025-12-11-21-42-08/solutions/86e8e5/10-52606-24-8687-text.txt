    pairs = []
    for p in range(2, max_val+1):

            pairs.append((ratio, p, q, D, g))

**Why this answer is correct and the others are not**

1. **Finding the minimal \(f(N)\).**  
   For a given \(N\) we look for three distinct divisors \(a,b,c\) of some integer \(x\) with \(a+b+c=N\).  
   The smallest possible value of \(x\) is obtained when the two smaller divisors actually divide the largest one, i.e. we can write  
   \[
   a=\frac{x}{p},\qquad b=\frac{x}{q},\qquad c=x,
   \]
   with distinct integers \(p,q>1\). Then  
   \[
   N=x+\frac{x}{p}+\frac{x}{q}=x\Bigl(1+\frac1p+\frac1q\Bigr),
   \]
   so
   \[
   x=\frac{pq\,N}{pq+p+q}.
   \tag{1}
   \]
   For (1) to give an integer \(x\) we need the denominator
   \[
   D=\frac{pq+p+q}{\gcd(p,q)}
   \]
   to divide \(N\).  

2. **Applying the condition to \(N=M+c\).**  
   Since \(M=3^{2025!}\) is a huge power of 3, for any divisor \(D\) not divisible by 3 we have
   \[
   M\equiv 1\pmod D .
   \]
   Hence for such \(D\) the condition \(D\mid (M+c)\) is equivalent to  
   \[
   D\mid (c+1).
   \tag{2}
   \]
   If \(D\) is a multiple of 3 we must have
   \[
   c\equiv0\pmod{3^{\alpha}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad c+1\equiv0\pmod{d},
   \]
   where \(D=3^{\alpha}d\) with \(\gcd(d,3)=1\).

3. **The optimal pairs \((p,q)\).**  
   Ordering the pairs \((p,q)\) by the ratio
   \[
   r(p,q)=\frac{pq}{pq+p+q}= \frac1{1+\frac1p+\frac1q},
   \]
   the smallest ratios come from  

   \[
   (2,3),\ (2,4),\ (2,5),\ (2,6),\dots
   \]

   For each of our six values of \(c\) we search this ordered list until we find a pair whose corresponding \(D\) satisfies (2) (or the 3‑multiple condition). The first admissible pair gives the minimal possible \(x\), i.e. \(f(M+c)\).
