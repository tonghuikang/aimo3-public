Let's attempt to compute T(m) for small m by recursion.

Define a full binary tree with leaf set size n=2^m.

Consider the root: its left subtree has n/2 leaves, right subtree also n/2 leaves. For a given leaf labeling, the winner of the root match is the maximum element among all n leaves; it lies either in left or right subtree. Suppose it is in left subtree; then all leaves in left subtree must be ranked above all leaves in right subtree. Within left subtree, the relative order among its leaves is governed recursively by the same rules applied to its subtree, but now the maximum among left subtree is the global maximum. The right subtree's maximum is the second highest among all leaves? Actually not necessarily second highest; because the right subtree may have its own maximum that is second highest among all leaves, but could be lower.

Thus the permutation σ can be constructed as follows: Choose which side (left or right) contains the global maximum. That side's leaves will appear first in σ (ordered recursively). The other side's leaves will appear later (ordered recursively). So the structure yields a recursion: T(m) = 2 * (some count for left) * (some count for right)? But left and right subtrees have same size and symmetric. However, the recursion also accounts for which side contains the overall maximum: two choices (left or right). The ordering of leaves within each side follows the same rules as the tournament for that subtree (since matches within subtree happen before the root). However, there is an extra condition: the maximum in the side that appears earlier must be the global maximum. This condition is automatically satisfied because we consider the global maximum as the max among all leaves; whichever side contains it must be the one placed first. So the recursion essentially: to construct a valid permutation for whole tree, we need to pick a side (left or right) for global max, then recursively choose a valid permutation for the side containing global max (with its own internal structure), and a valid permutation for the other side (which cannot contain any element larger than the global max; but that's always true). However, does the ordering of the other side depend on which side has the global max? Possibly not, as the constraints within the other side are independent of the other side; they only depend on the relative maxima within that side.
