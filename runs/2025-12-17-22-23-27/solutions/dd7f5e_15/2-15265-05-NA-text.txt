- Then optionally verify condition by constructing β = sign^-1 * x^{k - s} * γ for some k. Choose any k (e.g., 0) and let l = k + d. Then α * (x^{-s}γ) = x^0(1 + x^d) = 1 + x^d. But α * β = x^k + x^{k+d}. That matches requirement (k,l). So condition satisfied.

Thus all such α are shifty.

Now we need to ensure that there are no additional α that are not captured by this classification: any α such that there exists β and k,l. This would imply that α divides (up to shift) x^d+1. That is a necessary condition: given that α*β = x^k (1+x^d), factorization in ℤ[x,x^{-1}], α must be a divisor of x^d+1 after dividing out a monomial factor. Since ℤ[x,x^{-1}] is a unique factorization domain (UFD), factoring is unique up to units. So α must be up to unit a product of irreducible factors from factorization of x^d+1. Since x^d+1 has factorization into cyclotomic polynomials Φ_{2m} for m|d. Thus α must be of the form ± x^s ∏_{m∈S} Φ_{2m} for some subset S of divisors of d. Since any such subset yields deg ≤ deg(α). Since α's support is limited to degree ≤8, deg(α) = s+deg(product). So product's degree ≤8 and s+deg ≤8.

Thus α must be of this form. Conversely any such α qualifies. So classification exhaustive.

Thus answer =266.
