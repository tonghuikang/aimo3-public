6. **Allow a monomial factor \(x^{g}\)** – once a subset \(S\) with total degree \(d\) has been fixed, the overall polynomial can be multiplied by any monomial \(x^{g}\) provided the total degree stays ≤ 8.  
   Since the degree contributed by the monomial is \(g\), we may choose  

   \[
   g=0,1,\dots,8-d,
   \]

   i.e. **\(9-d\)** possibilities for the shift.

7. **Take the overall sign into account** – the unit \(-1\) in \(\mathbb Z[x]\) also divides every binomial, so for each choice of \(S\) and \(g\) we obtain *two* different shifty functions, one with the plus sign and one with the minus sign.

8. **Putting everything together** – the total number of shifty functions is

   \[
   \sum_{d=0}^{8} 2\,(9-d)\,N_d .
   \]

   Substituting the values of \(N_d\) computed in step 5 gives  

   \[
   \begin{aligned}
   &2\bigl[ (9-0)N_0+(9-1)N_1+(9-2)N_2+(9-3)N_3+(9-4)N_4\\
   &\qquad\quad +(9-5)N_5+(9-6)N_6+(9-7)N_7+(9-8)N_8 \bigr] \\[2mm]
   &=2\bigl[9\cdot1+8\cdot1+7\cdot2+6\cdot2+5\cdot4+4\cdot4+3\cdot8+2\cdot8+1\cdot14\bigr]\\
   &=2\bigl[9+8+14+12+20+16+24+16+14\bigr]\\
   &=2\cdot133\\
   &=\boxed{266}.
