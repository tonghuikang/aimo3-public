For indices that are even but not a multiple of 2? That's contradictory: any even number is 2*m. So all even numbers appear as potential indices. But odd indices? They appear for x^n - 1, not for x^n + 1. So the factors of 1 + x^d are cyclotomic polynomials Φ_n where n is even. Indeed that's known: x^d + 1 = ∏_{n|2d, n∤ d} Φ_n(x). Those n are precisely the divisors of 2d that are not divisors of d. As reasoned, they are exactly the n such that the highest power of 2 dividing n is one higher than that dividing d.

Thus the only cyclotomic polynomials that can appear in 1 + x^d are those Φ_n where n is even (i.e., n has a factor 2). In fact, n must be of the form 2m where m divides d.

Thus each divisor D of 1 + x^d is a product of some of those Φ_{2m} (maybe with sign). So D's degree is sum of φ(2m) over selected m dividing d.
