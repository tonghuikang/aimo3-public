**Key insights**

1. Write the condition with generating polynomials.  
   For \(\alpha\in\mathcal F\) let \(A(x)=\sum_{i=0}^8\alpha(i)x^i\).  
   For \(\beta\) define \(G(x)=\sum_j\beta(j)x^{-j}\).  
   Then \(S_n(\alpha)\star\beta\) is the coefficient of \(x^n\) in \(A(x)G(x)\).

2. The requirement “\(=1\) for two shifts \(k\neq l\) and \(0\) otherwise’’ means  
   \[
   A(x)G(x)=x^k+x^l= x^k(1+x^{d}),\qquad d=l-k\neq0 .
   \]
   Multiplying by the unit \(\pm x^{-k}\) we may assume \(k=0\); thus we need
   \[
   A(x)H(x)=1+x^{d}
   \tag{1}
   \]
   for some integer \(d>0\) and some Laurent polynomial \(H(x)\in\mathbb Z[x,x^{-1}]\).

3. In \(\mathbb Z[x]\) the factorisation of \(1+x^{d}\) is
   \[
   1+x^{d}= \prod_{\substack{m\mid 2d\\ m\nmid d}}\Phi_m(x),
   \]
   where \(\Phi_m\) is the \(m\)-th cyclotomic polynomial.  
   Hence any divisor of \(1+x^{d}\) is a product of a subset of those \(\Phi_m\).

4. Since \(A(x)\) has only non‑negative exponents \(\le8\), after removing a unit
   \(\pm x^e\) we obtain a polynomial \(f(x)\) (constant term 1) with
   \(\deg f\le8\) that divides some \(1+x^{d}\).  
   Therefore \(f\) must be a product of cyclotomic polynomials \(\Phi_m\) with
   \(\phi(m)\le8\) (otherwise the degree would exceed 8).

5. For a factor \(\Phi_m\) to appear in a divisor of \(1+x^{d}\) we need
   \(m\mid2d\) but \(m\nmid d\).  This forces the 2‑adic valuation
   \(v_2(m)\) to be exactly one larger than \(v_2(d)\); consequently **all
   chosen \(m\) must have the same \(v_2\)**.  Thus we may select any subset
   of cyclotomic polynomials whose indices share the same power of 2.
