Given the geometry, each point p is contained in r =40 lines. For a given pair (p,q), there may be lines that contain both points (i.e., the unique line through p and q). In AG(4,3), any two distinct points lie on exactly one line. So there is exactly one line ℓ_{pq} that contains both p and q.

Thus lines containing p are 40 lines, lines containing q are 40 lines, with exactly one line common (the line through both). So total lines that contain either p or q is 40 + 40 - 1 = 79.

Thus each line either contains p only, q only, or both.

Now we need to count ordered quadruples of lines (ℓ1,...,ℓ4) such that the union of lines covers p and q.

Equivalently, each point must be covered by at least one line among the four. This condition is that the set of four lines is not entirely contained in the complement of lines covering p (i.e., at least one line is from the 40 lines containing p) and similarly for q.

Thus the number of ordered quadruples where both p and q are covered = total ordered quadruples minus those where p not covered minus those where q not covered plus those where neither p nor q covered (by inclusion-exclusion). Since lines covering p are the complement of lines not covering p.

Define L_p = set of lines containing p (size 40). Complement = L \ L_p (size 1080-40 = 1040). Similarly L_q = lines containing q.

Ordered quadruples where p not covered: all four lines from L \ L_p => (L - 40)^4 = 1040^4.

Similarly for q: also 1040^4.

Ordered quadruples where neither p nor q covered: lines from L \ (L_p ∪ L_q). L_p ∪ L_q is the set of lines containing p or q. Its size = 79 (since 40+40-1). So complement size = 1080 - 79 = 1001.

Thus ordered quadruples with neither p nor q covered = 1001^4.

Thus ordered quadruples where both p and q covered = total L^4 - 2*1040^4 + 1001^4.

Thus count per pair is same for any unordered pair (p,q). So total sum over p<q is C(81,2) times that.

Thus total contribution of double points:

sum_{p<q} count = C(81,2) * (L^4 - 2*1040^4 + 1001^4).
