We need to partition the 2^t participants into two groups of size 2^{t-1} each, winners and losers, such that there exists a perfect matching where each winner beats a loser. Since the faster runner always beats slower, this condition is simply that the set of winners and losers can be paired. But we can count the number of possible winner sets along with matchings. However, the recursion for a_t (orderings) will involve counting ways to choose winner/loser partition, then recursively ordering each subgroup (since after first round, winners and losers each independently go through remaining rounds). Moreover, the winners and losers subgroups retain relative speed order (they are subsets of original order). The final ordering of participants is determined by interleaving the orderings within winners and losers groups according to binary significance: Winners (bit 1) get higher scores than losers (bit 0). Thus the final ranking is: first all winners (sorted among themselves by their lower-order bits), then all losers (sorted similarly). Therefore, the final ordering can be constructed recursively: choose a partition (W, L) of participants into two equal sets that can be matched; order W recursively (giving ordering of winners), order L recursively; then final ranking concatenates the ordering of W before ordering of L.

Thus a_t = (number of valid partitions (W, L) with perfect matching) * a_{t-1}^2.

But note that partition (W, L) with perfect matching is not just any subset of size 2^{t-1} that can be matched; the pairing matters? Actually the winners and losers sets determine the ordering; the specific matching does not affect final scores because winners already have 1 bit and losers have 0; the particular loser each winner beats does not affect later rounds (since both groups continue independently). So the exact matching is irrelevant for the final ordering; only the partition matters (who is winner vs loser). However, there may be multiple matchings possible for a given partition; but since they all lead to same partition, the partition counts once. So the number of possible partitions that admit at least one matching is the number of "feasible subsets".

Thus a_t = c_t * (a_{t-1})^2, where c_t = number of feasible winner subsets of size 2^{t-1} among 2^t ordered participants.
