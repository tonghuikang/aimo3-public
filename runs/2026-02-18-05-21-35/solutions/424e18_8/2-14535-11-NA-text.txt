There is no restriction on having more '(' than ')'; that's allowed. The restriction is that you cannot have more ')' than '(' because a loser cannot be matched to an earlier winner? Actually a loser can be matched to any earlier winner (faster), so a ')' can be matched to a prior '('; that's okay. So you cannot have a ')' without a preceding '(' that is unmatched. That's exactly the standard condition: at any prefix, # ')' cannot exceed # '('.

Thus the condition is # losers ≤ # winners at any prefix scanning fast to slow. That's the opposite of earlier. Let's test with n=2: sequence fast, slow. Suppose fast is winner '(' and slow loser ')': prefix 1 (fast) has # '('=1, # ')' =0, okay. Prefix 2: both 1 each, okay.
