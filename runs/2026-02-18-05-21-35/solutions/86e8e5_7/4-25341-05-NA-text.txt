General problem: For given n, we want to minimize N such that there exist distinct divisors a,b of N with a+b = n - N. The maximum possible sum of two distinct proper divisors of N is S(N) = max_{a≠b|N} (a+b). For N divisible by 6, S(N) = N/2 + N/3 = (5/6)N. For N divisible by 4 but not 6, the two largest distinct divisors could be N/2 and N/4 => sum = (3/4)N. For N divisible by 3 but not 2, largest are N/3 and N/9 maybe sum = (4/9)N. So the ratio n/N = 1 + (a+b)/N = 1 + s where s ≤ max sum/N. So minimum ratio n/N is 1 + max_sum/N where max_sum/N is the maximum possible fraction of N contributed by two distinct divisors. This is at most 5/6 (achieved by N multiple of 6). Hence minimal n/N is 1 + 5/6 = 11/6. So ratio N/n ≥ 6/11. This is global lower bound. Achieving it requires N multiple of 6 and using N/2 and N/3.
