\(x^{c}\) with \(0\le c\le 8-d\); the resulting polynomial has its
support inside \(\{0,\dots ,8\}\).  This gives \(9-d\) different shifts.
Both signs \(\pm\) are allowed (changing the sign of \(\alpha\) and of
\(\beta\) simultaneously preserves (1)).  Consequently a divisor \(D\)
contributes  

\[
2\,(9-d)
\]

different functions \(\alpha\).

Summing over the 22 divisors we obtain  

\[
\sum_{D}2\,(9-\deg D)=2\Bigl[\,80\,\Bigr]=160 .
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### Why the other answers are wrong

* The count \(80\) omits the sign choice (each admissible \(\alpha\)
  also works with its negative).
* The count \(44\) (and the larger \(266\)) includes products of
  cyclotomic factors whose 2‑adic orders differ; such a product never
  divides any binomial \(1+x^{m}\) and therefore cannot arise from a
  shifty \(\alpha\).
