### 2.  Divisors of \(1+x^{d}\)

It is a standard fact that  

\[
1+x^{d}= \prod_{\substack{m\mid 2d\\m\not\mid d}}\Phi_{m}(x),
\]

where \(\Phi_{m}\) is the \(m\)-th cyclotomic polynomial.
If a cyclotomic \(\Phi_{m}\) occurs in the product, then  

\[
m\mid 2d\quad\text{and}\quad m\nmid d\;\Longrightarrow\;
v_{2}(m)=v_{2}(d)+1 . \tag{3}
\]

Consequently **all indices \(m\) that appear have the same 2‑adic valuation**.  
Therefore any divisor \(D\) of a binomial \(1+x^{d}\) is a product of *distinct*
\(\Phi_{m}\)’s whose indices \(m\) are **even** and share a common
\(v_{2}(m)\).

*Mixed‑valuation products are impossible.*  
For example, \(\Phi_{2}(x)\Phi_{4}(x)=(x+1)(x^{2}+1)\) would require
\(d\) odd (for \(\Phi_{2}\)) and even but not divisible by \(4\) (for
\(\Phi_{4}\)); no integer \(d\) satisfies both, so this product never
divides a binomial \(1+x^{d}\).
