**Why the other numbers are wrong**

*Solver 2 (114)* – Counts only cyclotomic factors whose index has exactly one factor 2 (the \(t=1\) group).  This excludes the even‑indexed cyclotomic polynomials \(\Phi_{4},\Phi_{8},\Phi_{12},\Phi_{16},\Phi_{20},\Phi_{24},\Phi_{30}\).  Those factors do give valid shifty functions (for example \(\alpha(x)=\Phi_{4}(x)=x^{2}+1\)), so 114 under‑counts.

*Solver 1 (266)* – Treats all admissible cyclotomic factors as independent and allows arbitrary mixtures.  A shifty \(\alpha\) must divide a binomial \(x^{k}+x^{l}=x^{e}(1+x^{d})\); all irreducible factors of \(1+x^{d}\) have the same 2‑adic valuation \(t=v_{2}(d)+1\).  Hence a legitimate \(\alpha\) can contain cyclotomic factors only from a single \(t\)‑group.  Mixing factors from different groups (e.g. \(\Phi_{2}\) together with \(\Phi_{4}\)) cannot divide a binomial, so the extra functions counted by 266 are not shifty.  Therefore 266 over‑counts.
