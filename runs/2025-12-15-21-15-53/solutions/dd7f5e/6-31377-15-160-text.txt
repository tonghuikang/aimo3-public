**Idea.**  
Write \(A(x)=\sum_{i=0}^8\alpha(i)x^i\).  
For any \(\beta\) let \(B(x)=\sum_{t}\beta(t)x^{-t}\). Then  

\[
S_n(\alpha)\star\beta=\text{coeff}_{x^n}\bigl(A(x)B(x)\bigr).
\]

Thus “shifty’’ means there are \(k\neq l\) and \(B\in\mathbb Z[x,x^{-1}]\) with  

\[
A(x)B(x)=x^k+x^l=x^l(1+x^{d}),\qquad d=k-l>0 .
\]

Write \(A(x)=x^{a}f(x)\) where \(a=\min\{i:\alpha(i)\neq0\}\) and \(f(0)\neq0\).  
Cancelling the monomial factor gives  

\[
f(x)C(x)=1+x^{d}
\]

for some \(C\in\mathbb Z[x]\). Hence \(f\) must be an integer divisor of \(1+x^{d}\).  
All divisors of \(1+x^{d}\) are products of distinct cyclotomic polynomials \(\Phi_{2e}\) (the even‑index ones), because  

\[
x^{d}+1=\prod_{e\mid d}\Phi_{2e}(x)
\]

and the factorisation is square‑free.  

Therefore a shifty \(\alpha\) is exactly  

\[
\alpha(x)=\pm x^{a}\!\!\prod_{i\in I}\Phi_{2e_i}(x)
\]

with \(a\ge0\), \(a+\deg\bigl(\prod\Phi_{2e_i}\bigr)\le8\).

**Key insight:**  
Write \(A(x)=\sum_{i=0}^8\alpha(i)x^i\) and \(B(x)=\sum\beta(j)x^j\).  
\(S_n(\alpha)\!\star\!\beta\) is the coefficient of \(x^n\) in \(A(x)B(x)\).  
Shifty ⇔ \(A(x)B(x)=x^k+x^l\) for some \(k\neq l\).  

Factor \(A(x)=x^a f(x)\) with \(f(0)\neq0\).  Then \(f(x)\mid x^d+1\) for some \(d>0\).  

**Cyclotomic factorisation:**  
\(x^d+1=\prod_{e\mid2d,\;e\nmid d}\Phi_e(x)\).  
Thus \(f\) is a product of distinct even‑indexed cyclotomic polynomials \(\Phi_{2m}\).  

**Compatibility condition:**  
For \(\Phi_{2m}\) to divide \(x^d+1\) we need \(v_2(d)=v_2(m)\) (same 2‑adic valuation) and the odd part of \(m\) divides the odd part of \(d\).  
Hence all \(\Phi_{2m}\) in a divisor must share the same \(v_2(m)\).
